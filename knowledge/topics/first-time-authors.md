## The right order of steps

Most first-book problems come from doing the right tasks in the wrong order. The usual sequence from a finished draft:
1. Decide formats (eBook, paperback or both), platforms (Amazon only or "wide"), ISBN approach, trim size and budget.
2. Edit, then lock the text (developmental edit, line edit, copyedit, then proofread). Text changes after formatting shift page breaks, the TOC and eventually the spine width.
3. Format the interior and record the final page count. (This is where PublishPro fits.)
4. Design the cover; the print wraparound waits for the final page count because the spine width depends on it.
5. Register the book: ISBNs, prices, description, keywords and categories.
6. Order a proof copy, check it, gather early reviews and launch.
A realistic planning range from finished draft to launch is about 14 to 20 weeks, faster for short, lightly edited books.

## Preparing a Word manuscript before uploading

Helpful but not required: use Word's Heading 1 style for chapter titles, avoid manual tab indents and repeated blank lines, and use page breaks between chapters. PublishPro detects structure and cleans irregular spacing and line breaks automatically, so the manuscript doesn't need to be pre-formatted.

## ISBN basics

- A standard Kindle eBook on KDP does not need an ISBN.
- A paperback and a hardcover each need their own ISBN.
- KDP's free ISBN works only on KDP and lists the publisher as "Independently published". It doesn't give Amazon your copyright.
- Buying your own ISBN (Bowker in the US; free or paid in other countries depending on the national agency) lets you use your own imprint name and the same print edition on KDP and IngramSpark.
- Guide: https://publishpro.app/blog/own-isbn-or-free-kdp-isbn-for-first-book

## KDP Select vs going wide

KDP Select (Kindle Unlimited) requires the eBook to be exclusive to Amazon for each 90-day enrollment. Going wide means selling through Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play Books and others too. Neither is better in every case.

## Good first questions to understand an author's situation

Is the manuscript finished and edited? Is it in Word or Google Docs? eBook, paperback or both? Which platforms? Fiction or non-fiction, and are there images? Ask one at a time, only when it helps.
