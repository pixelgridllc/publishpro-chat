## Short answer

KDP is the simpler and usually more profitable choice if most sales will come from Amazon. IngramSpark is better if you want bookstores and libraries to be able to order the book, because you set the wholesale discount and can accept returns. Many indie authors use both: KDP for Amazon and IngramSpark for everything else, using the same ISBN they own on each. PublishPro's Standard Book plan produces print-ready cover files for both KDP and IngramSpark.

## KDP details (October 2026 snapshot)

Paperback royalty on Amazon.com: 60% of list price minus printing when priced at $9.99 or more, 50% below that. Kindle eBook: 70% royalty band from $2.99 to $12.99 on Amazon.com (minus a delivery fee). Expanded Distribution reaches other retailers and libraries at 40% minus printing, and hardcovers aren't eligible. KDP gives no control over discount or returns, which bookstores care about. KDP's setup, cover calculator and Print Previewer are beginner-friendly.

## IngramSpark details

Ingram is one of the world's largest book distributors, so bookstores and libraries can order from its catalog. You choose the wholesale discount (around 40% is common for online-only; bookstores generally expect more) and whether to accept returns. Royalty is list price minus your discount, printing cost and a distribution fee. File rules are stricter and there are more settings. IngramSpark supports eBook, paperback and hardcover including dust jackets.

## Other eBook stores

Apple Books, Kobo Writing Life, Google Play Books and Barnes & Noble Press accept EPUB directly; aggregators such as Draft2Digital and Smashwords distribute to many stores at once. BookBaby and Lulu are other self-publishing services. PublishPro's EPUB is compatible with these.
